# U-Calm Aviation — Services & Offerings

The operational catalogue, read from the member's side of the relationship. This document describes *what U-Calm Aviation delivers to members, how aviation sits inside the concierge relationship, how it is packaged and priced in architecture (not in rates), and where it connects to everything else U-CALM arranges*.

It is the second-strongest filter after the brand canvas: if a proposal cannot be mapped to a service line in this document, it either needs a new service defined here — or it does not belong in the U-Calm Aviation offering.

This file is for internal teams, the concierge desks, sales, product, and any AI tool generating proposal or itinerary copy. Do not publish externally without the Managing Director's sign-off.

---

## 1. Service architecture in one page

U-Calm Aviation is **aviation as a service line inside U-CALM membership**. It is not a product sold separately. Every member's aviation activity flows through the concierge channel they already use.

### The three operational channels (shared with the sister brand, wrapped in concierge)

1. **Aviation.** The flight itself — charter, on-demand, group, positioning opportunities when they exist.
2. **Destination Management.** Everything on the ground at both ends — FBO handling, cars, accommodation, restaurant, household, medical, local fixer — coordinated with the rest of the member's itinerary.
3. **Executive Protection.** Security planning, close protection, secure ground, advance party — coordinated through the same partner and the same concierge.

### The single commercial construct

U-Calm Aviation is **one construct only: U-CALM membership with aviation included as a service line**. There is no on-demand-without-membership, no jet card, no pay-per-flight tier for non-members. A member can certainly fly once a year, or twice, or weekly — but the member is always a member first, and the flight arrives inside the membership.

Billing is always on the U-CALM member statement. No separate invoice ever. No separate direct debit. No separate login.

### Where aviation sits in the wider concierge catalogue

Aviation is one line in a longer list that includes restaurants, household staffing, private medical, event curation, child-related logistics, property management, fine-art and watch acquisition coordination, and travel curation. Members do not see a *U-Calm Aviation product page*; they see aviation as a category within their concierge relationship.

## 2. Aviation — the operational spine, concierge-wrapped

The aviation operation is delivered to full aviation-house standards. The concierge is the interface; the discipline behind it is aviation-grade.

### 2.1 Member Charter

Point-to-point private aviation arranged through the concierge, sourced from a vetted operator panel, tailored to the mission.

**What is included**
- Aircraft matched to the mission (range, cabin, crew configuration) — chosen by the aviation desk, proposed to the member by their concierge.
- Operator vetted against published aviation-house safety standards (ARG/US-, IS-BAO-, Wyvern-equivalent where applicable).
- Crew briefed on the member's preferences already on file (catering, language, pets, privacy, children, family patterns).
- FBO arrangement at both ends, tied into the wider itinerary.
- Flight plan, slot coordination, and regulatory filings handled behind the scenes; the member never sees this register.
- The concierge remains the single named point of contact — the aviation specialist is audible only when the member asks, or when technical detail is specifically requested.

**Aircraft categories available**

| Category | Typical examples | Range | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light jet | Citation CJ3, Phenom 300 | Up to 3 hr | Short European hops — a lunch in Milan, a meeting in Zurich |
| Midsize jet | Citation XLS+, Hawker 900XP | 3–4 hr | London–Athens, London–Istanbul, regional family movements |
| Super-midsize | Challenger 350, Citation Longitude | 4–6 hr | Transatlantic edge, full-Europe movements with cabin room |
| Heavy jet | Falcon 7X, Gulfstream G500 | 6–10 hr | Europe–East Coast US, London–Dubai, family-scale missions |
| Ultra-long-range | Global 7500, G650ER, Falcon 10X | 10–16 hr | Non-stop global for principals whose routes demand it |
| Turboprop / STOL | King Air 350, PC-12 | Up to 2 hr | Mountain, island, and short-runway destinations (Samedan, Courchevel, island hops) |
| VIP rotor | AW139, H145 | Up to 90 min | Alpine ski transfers, harbour arrivals, yacht-to-shore |

We do not own fleet. We source. The concierge will propose one recommended aircraft with a brief rationale — not a menu. The member's preferences on file drive the rationale; they are not re-asked.

**Lead time:** 24–72 hours preferred; a four-hour notice window on eligible aircraft for enrolled members (see §2.2).

**Typical price architecture:** flight hour × aircraft category × route × seasonality, plus positioning (if any), plus handling and fuel surcharges. All-in figure on the member statement, no itemised charter invoice delivered separately.

### 2.2 Short-notice aviation — the four-hour window

A documented, operationally-tested capability: for eligible aircraft, routes, and enrolled members, the aviation desk will lift within four hours of the member's concierge confirming approval.

**How it works (from the member's side)**
- The member WhatsApps or emails their concierge: *"I need to be in Geneva this afternoon."*
- The concierge (not the member) coordinates with the aviation desk. Quote returned to the concierge within 15 minutes, presented to the member within a further few minutes in the concierge's voice.
- Member confirms. Aircraft tail locked. Crew briefed. Wheels-up within four hours of confirmation. Ground is on the itinerary already.

**Eligibility**
- Active U-CALM membership with aviation enrolment (pre-captured passport, preferences, emergency contacts, ground patterns).
- Origin and destination on the operational map (held internally, kept current).
- Weather, slot, and crew-duty windows permit.
- Not available for the member's first-ever flight — onboarding must be complete.

**What this is not:** a marketing claim. The four-hour window is an operational discipline. When we cannot meet it for a specific request, the concierge says so in the first message, and proposes the closest alternative.

### 2.3 Group and family missions

Multi-aircraft movements coordinated to a single itinerary — family reunions, intergenerational trips, wedding parties, milestone birthdays, a child's year-out travel, staff movements.

**What is included**
- A single concierge-led project line, with a named aviation project manager in support.
- Aircraft pairing matched to group size, route, and discretion profile.
- Synchronous or staggered arrivals as the family's rhythm requires.
- FBO capacity confirmed at destination.
- Baggage, equipment, and dietary coordination across the group.
- Staff accommodation, household logistics, and ground tied into the same itinerary.
- One invoice line on the member statement at mission close.

### 2.4 Positioning opportunities (quiet benefit)

The aviation operation maintains a rolling list of short-notice positioning opportunities — legs where the aircraft is already moving. When one of these aligns with a member's known travel pattern, the concierge may surface it privately as *"we have a morning aircraft repositioning into Nice on Thursday; would this be useful to you?"*.

These are **never public**, **never advertised**, **never framed as a sale**. They appear as quiet value inside an existing relationship. A member who takes one pays a member-aligned rate; one who doesn't is not made to feel they have missed anything.

## 3. Destination Management — ground, woven into the itinerary

For U-Calm Aviation, ground is not the second half of the product — it is the *continuity* that makes aviation feel unseparated. The concierge who arranges the restaurant on arrival is the same concierge who arranged the flight.

### 3.1 Ground transport
- Chauffeured cars at both ends — vetted, insured, discreet, with drivers known by name to the concierge.
- Arrival timing tied to wheels-down, not to the published ETA. The car is waiting; the member is not.
- Fleet matched to cargo, party size, children-in-seat requirements, and discretion profile. Armoured available where EP has advised.
- Drivers briefed by the concierge on route alternatives, local congestion, and any small detail the member has asked for in the past (a specific kiosk for a newspaper, a florist stop, the pharmacy on the way home).

### 3.2 Accommodation
- Hotel bookings at U-CALM's preferred properties and the member's own preferred properties where those are on file.
- Private villa and chalet access — Gstaad, St. Moritz, Ibiza, Mykonos, Mustique, Monaco, Provence, Porto Cervo, and others.
- Household staff coordinated in parallel — the housekeeper briefed, the fridge stocked, the linens turned.
- Staff accommodation for the member's own travelling team is arranged separately and invisibly.

### 3.3 Restaurant, table, and event
- Reservation, private dining, off-menu arrangement, by the same concierge who made the reservations that sat around the member's last dinner at home.
- Dietary, allergen, religious, child, and age-appropriate requirements carried forward from the member's profile without re-asking.
- Event-scale venues — private museum, gallery, yacht berth, box seat, theatre box — where the member's pattern warrants.

### 3.4 Medical and contingency
- Pre-flight medical clearance, where required, arranged through the household doctor or U-CALM's medical partners.
- On-ground medical on standby for specific missions, particularly where a member is recovering, expecting, or travelling with a vulnerable family member.
- Evacuation and repatriation coordinated through pre-agreed insurers and the household's existing arrangements.

### 3.5 Local fixers and language
- Named fixer contacts in every signature destination, known to the concierge by first name.
- Language cover — English, French, German, Italian natively; others by arrangement.
- Cultural briefings and small courtesies handled silently — the right greeting, the right tip format, the right car colour, the correct side of the street for the drop-off.

**Price architecture:** coordination fee absorbed into the membership; pass-through costs shown as itemised lines on the member statement at month-end. No hidden markup.

## 4. Executive Protection — the third channel, the same partner

The member's protection, where required, is coordinated with the flight through the same partner the sister aviation brand uses — integrated into the concierge relationship so that the protection lead and the concierge are already talking before the member asks.

### 4.1 Services
- **Risk assessment** — mission-specific, written, confidential, shared only with those named by the principal.
- **Close protection** — one to four operatives per principal; low-profile or overt profile as the member requires.
- **Secure ground** — armoured transport, counter-surveillance drivers, advance party.
- **Venue and residence checks** — hotel room sweeps, residence security surveys.
- **Family protection** — minors, pets, household staff, elderly family members.
- **Travel-risk intelligence** — rolling briefings for the destinations the member visits regularly.

### 4.2 When protection is triggered
- Mandatory assessment for destinations flagged on the internal risk matrix.
- Optional for all other missions; always offered during onboarding.
- Pre-existing household detail or family-office protection teams are integrated; U-Calm Aviation's protection can lead or support as required.

### 4.3 What is not offered
- Kidnap-and-ransom response (referred to specialist insurers via the household or family office).
- Armed protection in jurisdictions where it is illegal.
- Cyber-protection beyond travel-specific device hygiene for the mission.

**Price architecture:** day rate per operative, plus advance-party days and equipment hire. Fully quoted in advance, absorbed into the member statement.

## 5. Onboard — what the cabin contains

U-Calm Aviation does not operate aircraft. We do, however, specify the onboard details the concierge has on the member's file.

- **Catering** — curated from pre-approved suppliers against the member's profile. Dietary, allergen, religious, child-appropriate requirements travel with the file. English, French, Italian, Japanese, halal, kosher, vegan, ayurvedic — all arrangeable where requested in advance.
- **Cabin linen and amenity** — supplied to the U-CALM preferred specification where the operator permits.
- **Inflight reading and entertainment** — downloaded, pre-loaded, subtitled if requested. The member's newspaper of choice is onboard. A specific book the member ordered can be waiting on the seat.
- **Connectivity** — Wi-Fi status published per aircraft; if unreliable, the concierge flags it before approval.
- **Pets** — pet travel protocols documented; always declared to the operator in advance; temperature, water, bedding, and transit box handled.

**What we will not claim:** any onboard health or physiological benefit. No *"arrive restored"*, no *"cabin altitude optimised"*, no *"HEPA-filtered wellness cabin"*, no *"onboard sleep coach"* programming. A member who wants any of those things can have the specific arrangement made for their specific mission, once, because they asked — not as a marketed product. See `07-Voice-and-Messaging.md` §6.4 for the forbidden vocabulary.

## 6. Membership architecture — aviation as a service line

U-Calm Aviation is not a standalone tiered product. Aviation is included inside U-CALM's existing membership tiers. The member's aviation usage, rather than placing them in an "aviation tier", simply informs the balance of services delivered within their membership.

**Illustrative guidance (U-CALM membership held throughout):**

| Aviation usage profile | How aviation sits in the member's year |
|---|---|
| **Occasional** — fewer than 20 flight hours a year | Aviation appears as one line among many in the concierge relationship. Quoted by mission. No dedicated aviation enrolment required. |
| **Regular** — 20–80 flight hours a year | Full aviation enrolment during onboarding — passport, preferences, ground patterns. Four-hour notice window available on eligible aircraft. Member rates applied. Quarterly aviation review added to the concierge review cadence. |
| **High-frequency** — 80–200 flight hours a year | Dedicated aviation specialist paired with the member's concierge. Monthly aviation reporting inside the concierge update. Flight-hour banking available. Integrated protection liaison. Signature-destination residences and fixers kept on warm retainer. |
| **Intensive** — 200+ flight hours a year | Two concierges (primary + back-up) plus two aviation specialists. 24/7 global cover already in place through U-CALM. Destination management included. Signature-destination residences on retainer. Quarterly in-person reviews combining concierge and aviation. |

Members move between profiles in conversation with their concierge — never by automated email. The membership itself does not change.

## 7. Pricing philosophy

U-Calm Aviation's pricing architecture is the operational expression of the brand.

1. **Transparent.** All-in figures, shown on the U-CALM member statement. No positioning-leg surprises. Fuel surcharges, landing fees, catering, and handling shown as itemised lines where the member asks.
2. **Clean.** No hidden margins stacked on pass-through costs. Destination management and protection are pass-through plus a stated coordination fee, absorbed into the statement.
3. **Honest about sourcing.** Where a smaller aircraft is genuinely better value for a mission, the concierge recommends it — even when it reduces revenue. The brand does not earn on up-sized aircraft.
4. **Priced to hold.** U-Calm Aviation does not discount quotes. Member rates are defensible, published internally, and applied consistently.
5. **Never commoditised.** The aviation offer is not a quote-fight. If another provider's rate is lower, the concierge can explain the difference; the brand does not chase.
6. **Never brochured publicly.** Pricing does not appear in public-facing materials. It appears on the member statement, where it belongs.

Actual rates, margins, and floor prices are published in the internal operations manual, not here.

## 8. What U-Calm Aviation will not do

A brand is defined as much by its refusals as its offerings. U-Calm Aviation will not:

- Charter aircraft from operators that do not meet the published safety verification.
- Fly passengers whose identity we cannot verify (no anonymous missions).
- Sell jet-cards, fractional shares, or pre-paid-hour programmes — they contradict the *aviation-inside-membership* architecture.
- Operate a marketplace model or a self-serve inventory.
- Name a member, a route, or an aircraft publicly.
- Advertise on price, on social media, or through loyalty programmes.
- Provide aviation services into jurisdictions under active sanction.
- Market "wellness aviation" of any description — no cabin-altitude claims, no HEPA claims, no onboard practitioner programmes, no *arrive restored* language. (See `01-Brand-Story-and-Origin.md` §4.3.)
- Refer externally to the sister aviation brand.
- Take missions where the purpose is inconsistent with the brand's ethical standards (case-by-case; final call with Managing Director).

These refusals are features, not gaps.

## 9. Onboarding — the first 30 days

### 9.1 Day 0 — Introduction
The member is already a U-CALM member; U-Calm Aviation is introduced as a service line of the existing concierge relationship. The concierge briefs the aviation desk on the member's profile. A named aviation specialist is assigned as invisible support to the concierge.

### 9.2 Day 1–7 — Pre-flight setup
Passport data, pet profiles, medical notes, payment arrangements already held by U-CALM — aviation inherits them. Home-base geography confirmed. Signature destinations captured. Any protection pattern is shared with the protection partner.

### 9.3 Day 8–30 — First mission (if any)
If a mission is flown in the first 30 days, it is delivered to full aviation-house standard with heightened post-mission review. The concierge debriefs the member; the aviation desk debriefs the concierge. Nothing about this process is visible to the member.

### 9.4 Day 30 — Quiet review
A short note from the concierge: *We're glad to have your aviation inside your U-CALM relationship. Here's what we've put in place; here's what we're watching for.* No tier upsell. No up-pitch.

## 13. Operator discipline — the hidden standard

*Credit: Fly Victor operator discipline, translated into concierge register.* The concierge never discusses operators with the member; the discipline sits behind the voice, invisible.

### 13.1 The aviation desk's five-filter check

Every operator on the sourcing panel passes all five. No exceptions.

1. **Safety rating** — ARG/US Gold or Platinum, IS-BAO Stage 2+, or Wyvern Wingman. Current certification.
2. **Insurance hull and liability** — policy limits documented, insurer named, period current.
3. **Operator AOC and registry** — AOC jurisdiction confirmed, aircraft registry checked, no adverse regulatory events within 24 months.
4. **Crew standards** — pilot hours, type ratings, recurrent training, English proficiency, and the specialist-airport certifications required for LCY, SMV, CVF, SBH.
5. **Operational track record with the desk** — three-mission heightened monitoring for first-flight operators; incidents logged against the operator record.

### 13.2 The operator brief for every mission

The aviation desk sends the operator a mission brief including aircraft, crew, FBO, a redacted member profile (preferences, language, pets, mobility, privacy), ground handover, weather, and slot status. The member never sees this document; the concierge does, and uses it to anticipate.

### 13.3 Post-mission debrief — invisible to the member

Within 48 hours of every mission, the aviation desk writes a structured note: what worked, what slipped, what goes into the member profile, what goes into the operator record. The concierge reads the note; only the member-relevant items surface in the next concierge conversation.

### 13.4 First-flight operators

An operator the desk has not flown with before runs under heightened monitoring for the first three missions and is not used for members in the High-frequency or Intensive aviation usage profiles until they have cleared that window.

## 14. Disruption protocols — the concierge narrates

*Credit: Fly Victor disruption desk, translated into concierge register.* Weather, slots, crew, and mechanics will disrupt missions. For U-Calm Aviation members, the response is a single voice — the member's concierge — narrating calmly what has already been rebuilt.

### 14.1 Delay — under two hours
- Aviation desk notifies the concierge first. Ground is rebooked before any message reaches the member.
- Concierge sends a single WhatsApp in the concierge voice (see `07-Voice-and-Messaging.md` §8.10): new time, rebuilt ground, nothing else.
- The message announces fixes, not problems.

### 14.2 Delay — over two hours, or same-day cancellation
- Alternative aircraft sourced in parallel with the first member message going out.
- Where the alternative is equivalent, it is presented as a continuity move, not a downgrade.
- Where the alternative costs materially more and the member did not cause the disruption, the difference is absorbed into the membership; no surcharge language appears anywhere.

### 14.3 Aircraft change (tail swap)
- Confirmed with the operator before the concierge writes to the member.
- New tail, new crew names, ETA carried over unchanged unless physics forbid.
- Configuration (seating, pets, cargo, catering) carried forward.
- The concierge tells the member what changed and what did not, in that order — emphasising continuity (see §8.10 of the voice doc).

### 14.4 Weather, slot, and NOTAM disruption
- Watched by the aviation desk from T-24, restated at T-12, alerted at T-6 on material change.
- The concierge's pre-brief (§8.10 of the voice doc) absorbs any revised times; the member never hears of a change for the first time through a flight-tracking app.
- For weather-volatile destinations (Samedan, Courchevel, Mykonos in August), a Plan B airport is written into the brief at T-24; the member is told only if the Plan B is invoked.

### 14.5 Medical or member-initiated change
- Warmth is loudest here. No surcharge language in the first reply. No operator-side blame. The member's circumstance is the only subject of the first message.
- The operational rebuild happens in the background; the member receives a single message per material change, in the concierge's voice.

### 14.6 What a disruption must never become
- A news item. No member identity, operator name, or destination ever leaks to any public channel.
- A blame loop. The operator's mistake is logged internally (see §13), not narrated to the member.
- A moment the member feels in any way alone. The concierge is audible; the aviation machinery is not.
