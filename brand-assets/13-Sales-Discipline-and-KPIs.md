# U-Calm Aviation — Concierge Discipline & KPIs

**Internal only. Do not share externally. Do not paste excerpts into member-facing copy without rewriting through the voice document first. In particular: the KPIs in this document are not to be described to members in any form — the member experiences a concierge relationship, not a measured service.**

This file is the internal operating discipline for the U-Calm Aviation desk and the concierges who sit over it. It defines the concierge cadence, the member-facing metrics translated from broker-side discipline, the operator hygiene shared with the Ascent operation, and the belt-and-braces checks that sit behind the calm voice documented in `07-Voice-and-Messaging.md` and the services catalogue in `08-Services-and-Offerings.md`.

**Credit.** The framework in this document draws on the Fly Victor sales methodology, shared with JP during his training with their desk. Fly Victor is a dotted-line back-end broker partner to the aviation operation. For U-Calm Aviation, the methodology has been **translated into concierge register** — this is the essential move. The underlying discipline is the same (cadence, measurement, operator hygiene, disruption protocol); the metrics are renamed and the voice is softened because we are not brokers and our members are not quotes.

This attribution is internal; the discipline itself is now part of the U-Calm Aviation operating system and is never described externally as "a Fly Victor method."

---

## 1. Why this document exists

U-Calm Aviation's external register is warm, composed, and unhurried. Internally, that calm is the product of a concierge cadence that does not slip, an operator panel (shared with the sister operation) that is vetted and re-vetted, and a disruption protocol that is rehearsed.

If the external voice is *what the member hears*, the contents of this file are *what the concierge and the aviation desk do* behind the scenes so the voice remains true. Without this layer, every pillar in §2 of the voice document is aspirational. With it, Continuity, Anticipation, Warmth, Composure, and Restraint are measurable.

The broker-side language in the Fly Victor matrix ("quote", "close-out", "win rate", "cadence compliance") does not cross into the member experience. Everything here has been translated so that the *discipline* is kept and the *vocabulary* is concierge-appropriate. When in doubt, ask: *would I say this to the member?* If no, it belongs in this document and nowhere else.

## 2. Concierge cadence — by trip horizon

Every itinerary enters one of three trip-horizon tiers at issue. Cadence is set at that moment and does not relax.

| Horizon | Trip timing | First continuity check | Second | If silence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Near** | < 14 days | 24–36 hours after itinerary | 48–72 hours | gentle call at quote-expiry; no chase language |
| **Mid** | 2–6 weeks | 3–4 days | 10–14 days | call at T-14 days with refreshed itinerary |
| **Far** | 6+ weeks | 10 days | at T-30 days with refreshed arrangement | annual-planning note (see §4.5 / prompt 15.1) |

### Rules that hold across all horizons

- **Itinerary acknowledgement** (warmth first, hold-time second) is sent within 30 minutes of a concierge's itinerary landing in the member's inbox. The target is >85% acknowledgement rate — a concierge-voiced equivalent of the broker-side receipt confirmation (see §3).
- **Each continuity check must carry a new fact** — an updated restaurant booking, a fresh ground time, a weather note on the destination — not a repeated question. A repeat is worse than silence; it signals desk-filler and breaks the concierge illusion.
- **Each check is shorter than the last.** First ≤ 90 words; second ≤ 60; any third ≤ 40.
- **There is no close-out language.** The concierge does not "close" a member. If the member goes quiet, the concierge holds the possibility and re-surfaces it in the next seasonal touch (§4.5 / §7.1).
- **Silence past quote-expiry triggers an annual planning note**, not a final-chance email. The relationship outlasts the arrangement.

### Horizon escalation inside seasonal windows

Inside the five demand windows defined in `08-Services-and-Offerings.md` §12 (Eid/Easter, Summer peak, Asia peaks, Festive, Major events), every horizon moves forward by one. Far becomes Mid; Mid becomes Near. The concierge's anticipation on behalf of the member must be correspondingly earlier.

## 3. KPIs — concierge-translated, thresholds and bands

Six metrics are run at the aviation desk weekly. Each has a danger line, an acceptable band, and a strong band. These are internal concierge-operation metrics; none of them is visible to the member. Below the danger line: immediate conversation with the concierge and the aviation specialist. Inside the acceptable band: normal review. Above the strong band: document the pattern and share across the desk.

| KPI (concierge-translated) | Broker-side analogue | Danger | Acceptable | Strong | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Itinerary Acknowledgement Rate** (WhatsApp acknowledgements ÷ itineraries issued) | Receipt Confirmation Rate | < 55% | 70–85% | > 90% | Whether the concierge's itinerary was seen and the thread is live. |
| **Concierge Response SLA** (time from member message to concierge reply) | Follow-up SLA | > 60 min in hours; > 90 min out-of-hours | 15–30 min in hours | < 15 min in hours | Responsiveness of the concierge channel — the single most visible operation metric the member experiences. |
| **Continuity-Check Compliance** (% of live arrangements where the horizon's continuity check was honoured on time) | Cadence Compliance | < 75% | 85–95% | 100% | Internal discipline. Slippage here is the first sign the desk is overloaded and the concierge is falling back to firefighting. |
| **Member-Satisfaction Marker** (qualitative, captured at post-mission debrief + quarterly review) | Win Rate (translated) | < 4/5 average; any 2/5 | 4.2–4.6/5 | > 4.7/5 | Concierge-felt quality of the relationship. Qualitative, weighted toward long-form notes, not a Net Promoter score. |
| **Arrangement-Hold Coverage** (arrangements held / projected seasonal demand, by member segment) | Coverage Ratio | < 1.2× | 1.4–1.8× | > 1.8× | Whether the concierge has enough forward holds in place for the members' known patterns; not a pipeline measure — a continuity measure. |
| **Operator Discipline Log** (shared with Ascent — recorded missteps per operator, rolling 12 months) | Operator Discipline | ≥ 3 | 1–2 | 0 | Operator register quality. Shared with the Ascent desk; see §5. |

### What the KPIs do not measure

- The *qualitative* texture of a long-standing concierge relationship. That lives in the member profile notes and in the quarterly review conversation, not on a dashboard.
- The cost of the concierge's anticipation — forward holds that the member did not ultimately use, quiet restaurant reservations that were released, the florist contact maintained across years. These are investments in continuity, not costs to be minimised.
- The success of a graceful non-arrangement. A member who declines an itinerary because the concierge correctly read a hesitation is a signal of relationship strength, not a lost arrangement.

The desk reads KPIs with this context. Managing only to the numbers collapses the concierge relationship into a broker relationship.

### Weekly review rhythm

- **Monday 09:00 BST** — aviation desk + concierge desk standup. Itinerary acknowledgements, continuity-check compliance, any arrangements at risk of slipping this week. 20 minutes. No slides.
- **Friday 16:00 BST** — desk close. Week's member moments, operator log review, seasonality horizon for the next two weeks, quiet arrangements that have paid off for members.
- **First Friday of the month** — half-day operator review (shared with the Ascent desk; see §5). Member-satisfaction markers reviewed at quarterly rather than monthly cadence.

## 4. Concierge cadence — the five operational moves

The cadence in §2 is expressed through five operational moves. Each has a corresponding prompt in `12-AI-Prompt-Templates.md` §15. Note: U-Calm Aviation does not use the broker-side *proposal follow-up* or *quote-expiry close-out* moves directly — they have no concierge-appropriate analogue. The moves here are all concierge-voiced.

### 4.1 Itinerary acknowledgement (WhatsApp, within 30 min of itinerary)
Warmth first, hold-time second. Opens the thread, proves the itinerary is live, buys a hold without the member asking. The equivalent of the broker's receipt confirmation — but the concierge is not confirming receipt of a quote; she is holding a possibility.

### 4.2 Continuity check (WhatsApp or email, by horizon)
Carries a new fact. Never a repeat. Tone matches §8.9 of the voice document. Frequently interweaves with non-aviation touches — a restaurant confirmation, a household note, a school-diary reminder — so that aviation never stands alone as a thread.

### 4.3 Seasonal / annual planning note (email)
For members whose patterns imply travel in an approaching window (§7 of this doc; §12 of services doc). Proactive, not reactive. Carries one forward-looking prompt — not a check-in.

### 4.4 Delay / aircraft-change / pre-brief (WhatsApp)
The three disruption moves — delivered in concierge voice, not ops voice (see `07-Voice-and-Messaging.md` §8.10). Always: what changed → what did not → what is already rebuilt around the change.

### 4.5 Annual planning note (email, once a year per member, typically January)
Independent of any active arrangement. The concierge surfaces the coming year's seasonal windows against the member's known patterns and asks whether to hold specific possibilities. Light. Door-open. Carries no pitch.

Each move has its own prompt in `12-AI-Prompt-Templates.md` §15.1–15.5. Concierges should not redraft these from scratch; the voice is held by the prompts, not by the desk's memory.

## 5. Operator register — shared discipline with the sister operation

The services document §13 defines the five-filter operator check. The operator register is shared with the Ascent desk — one operation, two faces. This section covers how the register is maintained in respect of U-Calm Aviation members specifically.

### 5.1 The three operator states (unchanged from the shared register)

- **Active.** Passed all five filters; no logged incidents in the rolling 12 months, or one minor incident with documented remediation.
- **Review.** Three or more logged incidents in the rolling 12 months, or one material incident. Cannot be used for members in the High-frequency or Intensive aviation usage profiles; must be signed off by the ops lead for any member mission.
- **Suspended.** Five logged incidents or one disqualifying event. Removed from sourcing until an internal review closes — minimum 90 days and a documented correction.

### 5.2 What counts as a logged incident (concierge-inflected)

In addition to the shared incidents in the Ascent register (safety, AOC, crew standards, slot coordination, brief discipline), the U-Calm Aviation desk logs an incident for any mission in which the *concierge illusion* was broken — that is, where the member had to speak to anyone other than the concierge because the aviation operation did not hold its end.

- Operator-initiated call to the member without concierge brief.
- Crew or FBO staff who asked the member for information already on file.
- Tail-number change inside 24 hours that surfaced to the member first.
- Any moment the member felt handed over to "the aviation side" rather than held by the concierge.

These are concierge-specific incidents; they do not appear in the Ascent register, but they do count for operator state.

### 5.3 Incident-to-register flow

1. Concierge or aviation specialist writes the incident note in the mission debrief within 48 hours.
2. Aviation desk lead reviews and classifies weekly.
3. Register state changes are made at the Friday close review, jointly with the Ascent desk lead.
4. Operators moved to Review or Suspended are notified in writing; the reasons are documented; the correction is defined.
5. Return to Active requires sign-off from the aviation desk lead, the Ascent desk lead, and the Managing Director.

### 5.4 First-flight operators — member safeguard

A first-flight operator is *never* used for a member in the High-frequency or Intensive aviation usage profiles. For Occasional and Regular members, a first-flight operator is only used after the concierge has been briefed and has signed off on the specific mission.

## 6. Belt-and-braces checklists

The checks that prevent the ordinary failures. Run them — no specialist is too senior.

### 6.1 Pre-proposal check (before the concierge presents the itinerary)

- [ ] Slot confirmed at both ends, or the concierge has a clean alternative to propose.
- [ ] Operator on Active register; not a first-flight operator for any High-frequency or Intensive member.
- [ ] Crew certification for any specialist airport in the route (LCY, SMV, CVF, SBH).
- [ ] Insurance hull and liability current for the tail being considered.
- [ ] Ground coordinated at both ends and pre-agreed with the member's concierge counterparts in the destination (housekeeping, restaurant, driver, doctor if relevant).
- [ ] Weather horizon checked at T-24 equivalent; Plan B airport noted in the brief for weather-volatile destinations.
- [ ] Pricing absorbed into the U-CALM member statement; no separate quote document crosses to the member.
- [ ] Itinerary reads cleanly against the voice document — concierge register throughout, no aviation-first language, no forbidden phrases, no wellness vocabulary (§6.4 of voice doc).

### 6.2 Pre-departure check (T-12 to T-3)

- [ ] Tail number confirmed and logged.
- [ ] Crew names confirmed and, if the member has asked to know them, included in the pre-brief.
- [ ] FBO at both ends confirmed; handover times synced to wheels-down.
- [ ] Member profile carried forward to the operator and the destination concierge (dietary, pets, language, privacy, mobility, children).
- [ ] Ground transport confirmed at both ends — the named driver at the origin, the named driver at the destination, the vehicle matched to profile.
- [ ] Slot and NOTAM re-checked.
- [ ] Pre-brief written in concierge voice and queued for the evening before or morning-of (§4.4 / prompt 15.4).
- [ ] Continuity note sent to any other service line with a hand in the trip (housekeeping, household, event, medical) — see `12-AI-Prompt-Templates.md` §7.

### 6.3 In-flight monitoring

- [ ] Concierge holds phone on until wheels-up.
- [ ] Aviation desk monitors flight-tracking through to wheels-down.
- [ ] Any delay >15 minutes triggers §4.4 / prompt 15.2 in concierge voice before the member notices.
- [ ] Ground at destination re-timed to wheels-down, not to published ETA.
- [ ] Destination concierge (housekeeper, restaurant, driver) receives a single co-ordinated update, not multiple calls.

### 6.4 Post-mission (within 48 hours)

- [ ] Debrief written by aviation specialist and concierge jointly — what was delivered, what required intervention, what goes to the member profile, what goes to the operator log.
- [ ] Member profile updated with any new preference learned — carried across every service line, not held in an aviation silo.
- [ ] Operator incident (if any) logged, classified, and shared with the Ascent register.
- [ ] Billing absorbed into the next U-CALM member statement; pass-through costs itemised where the member asks.
- [ ] Post-flight note to the member: only if something surprising happened; otherwise the next scheduled concierge touch absorbs it.

## 7. Seasonal-window reference — plan the year for the member, quietly

The five windows in `08-Services-and-Offerings.md` §12 drive the concierge's anticipation on the member's behalf. The desk holds this calendar on a shared canvas, refreshed quarterly.

### 7.1 Quote-ahead targets (translated: arrangement-ahead targets)

| Window | Begin forward-looking note to members | Confirm operators by | Slot requests submitted by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eid / Easter | T-90 days | T-45 | T-30 |
| Summer peak | T-120 days | T-60 | T-45 |
| Asia peaks | T-90 days | T-45 | T-30 |
| Festive | T-150 days | T-90 | T-60 |
| Major events | Event-dependent | See event playbook | Event-dependent |

Note: U-Calm Aviation runs festive and summer windows slightly earlier than the Ascent desk (150 days vs 120; 120 days vs 120 with earlier member contact). The concierge's forward-looking note lands before a member has thought about it themselves — that is the product.

### 7.2 Event playbook — fixed windows held for members with known patterns

Standing playbook for: Davos (WEF), Monaco Grand Prix, Cannes Film Festival, Royal Ascot, Wimbledon, Art Basel (Basel, Miami, Hong Kong), Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, Chinese New Year. Each playbook names the corridor, the slot constraint, the operator shortlist, and the concierge counterparts (house, restaurant, driver, fixer) at the destination.

### 7.3 Weather-volatile destinations — standing member-side protocols

For Samedan, Courchevel, Mykonos (August), Chambéry (winter weekends), and Ibiza (August): the concierge holds a Plan B airport and a concierge-voiced delay protocol. The member is told only if the Plan B is invoked. The destination-side concierge counterparts are briefed on Plan B at T-24 regardless, so they can absorb the change invisibly.
